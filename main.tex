\chapter{The Third Stance: Logic Is Neither Eternal Nor Conventional}

Western philosophy of logic has long oscillated between two poles. The first
is \emph{logical Platonism}: the laws of logic are eternal truths, prior to
the world and to any thinker (Frege carried this banner). The second is
\emph{conventionalism or psychologism}: logic is a human agreement, or a habit
of the brain --- the pole Frege and Husserl attacked so effectively that it
nearly vanished from twentieth-century philosophy.

This book proposes a third stance, parallel to the informationalism of the
parent canon:

\begin{principle}[The Third Stance]\Dr\label{prin:third}
Logic is the structure that necessarily emerges from the condition of a
\emph{finite knower on a discrete graph}. It is the admissible transport
grammar that any finite system must possess in order to bring retained
distinguishabilities into comparison at all.
\end{principle}

Against Platonism: there is no ``third realm'' of logical law --- there is one
stream of causally ordered difference, and logic is the reading of that stream
at the knower's $\tau_c$. Against psychologism: the derivation does not start
from empirical human psychology but from \emph{structural} finitude ---
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tot}}>0$, bounded bandwidth, $\tau_c>0$ --- constraints
that bind \emph{any} finite system, human, animal, or machine. This answers
Frege's classical objection: we do not reduce logic to how humans happen to
think; we elevate it to how any finite system \emph{must} handle difference.

\begin{falsifier}
Exhibit a system that performs contentful comparison with no admissible
grammar whatsoever. Then the third stance fails (cf.\ falsifiers U2/U3,
Appendix~C).
\end{falsifier}

\chapter{Why Logic Must Have a Genesis}

A standard logic textbook begins ``let $p$ be a proposition'' --- as if
propositions, negation, and implication fell from the sky together. The
question such books never ask is: \emph{what must already exist for there to
be a proposition at all?}

The parent canon answered this style of question for physics (mass is not
substance but inverse memory time; time is not a container but the total
sequence of asymmetric transitions). This book does the same for logic:

\begin{principle}[Birth Certificates]\Df
Every logical constant must exhibit its own birth certificate: the step of the
42-step genesis stream from which it emerges, and every bridge assumption it
carries.
\end{principle}

The principle has teeth because it can \emph{reject}: if a logical constant
requires actual infinity, direct access to the latent world $\theta$, or
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tot}}=0$, then it is at best an idealized projection,
not native logic of a finite knower. Chapter~7 shows that classical bivalence
falls exactly into this class.

\chapter{The Primordial Logic Stream (L-01 to L-04)}

\begin{axiom}[L-01: Identity and difference are born as a pair]\Ax
From the primordial axiom $\exists\,a,b:\ a\neq b$, the statement $a=a$ is
definable only against the background of $a\neq b$. Identity does not precede
difference; the two arise together as the first contrast.
\end{axiom}

Here we stand against Leibniz (identity of indiscernibles as a first
principle) and closer to Heraclitus and N\=ag\=arjuna (Chapters~16--17).

\begin{theorem}[L-02: Negation as exclusion]\Th\label{th:negation}
Given distinguishability (the first bit), ``not this'' becomes sayable:
$\neg$ is definable as \emph{exclusion within a field of alternatives}
$A_D$.
\end{theorem}

This is by birth a \emph{choice negation}: ``not $a$'' means ``some other
member of $A_D$'', not the absolute complement of a universe. Consequence:
the law of excluded middle is licensed only when $A_D$ has been exhaustively
enumerated --- and for a finite knower whose salient field satisfies
$S_A \subsetneq \Delta$, exhaustiveness is never guaranteed. This is the root
of our sympathy with intuitionism (Chapter~21) without Brouwer's mentalism.

\begin{theorem}[L-03: Directed implication]\Th
From causal asymmetry ($A{\to}B \neq B{\to}A$), implication is a
\emph{directed walk along causal chains}, not a static truth table. Material
implication ($p\to q \equiv \neg p \lor q$) is the projection of directed
implication onto a static table; the notorious ``paradoxes of material
implication'' are the price of that projection, not deep truths about
implication. \Dr
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[L-04: Inference as descent]\Dr\ + \fd\label{th:descent}
Inference is a countable path of Lyapunov descent toward consistency:
$\dot\varepsilon = -\Lambda A^{\!\top} W r$, where a proof is a path on the
graph. \textbf{Operational evidence (LTP1-C1):} under constant evidence,
$V(0)=0.654$ descends monotonically (zero violations) to $8.2\times10^{-26}$.
\end{theorem}

This is the first time in the programme that ``inference'' has an executable
mathematical meaning rather than a metaphor. Its precise status --- a
\emph{chosen reading} supported by a finite diagnostic, with alternatives not
excluded --- is recorded in the Arrow Ledger (Appendix~D).

\chapter{The Defeasibility Theorem (L-06)}

\begin{theorem}[Defeasibility]\Thstar\ (under B1) + \fd\label{th:defeasible}
The logic of a finite knower must be defeasible: every settled conclusion
structurally carries the status ``may require revision''.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tot}}>0$ (the parent programme's irreducible error
floor) implies the readout $\delta$ never coincides with the latent world
$\theta$; hence for every settled conclusion there exists a trajectory of
$\delta(t)$ under which that conclusion must revise. Non-monotonicity is
therefore not a defect of an inference system but the \emph{normal dynamics}
of any system tracking a world it cannot access directly.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Operational evidence (LTP1-C2):} a conclusion settled at
$+1.000$ revises to $-1.000$ when the evidence flips at $t=30$; $V$ spikes to
$1.876$ and re-descends to $2.9\times10^{-13}$. The ``change of mind'' of a
logical system is captured as pure dynamics, with no ad-hoc revision machinery
added (Chapter~21 compares AGM).

\begin{remark}[Hume relocated]\Dr
The problem of induction transforms: ``what justifies inference from past to
future?'' becomes ``under what bound does a tracking system operate?'' The
answer is input-to-state stability: bounded $\dot\delta$ implies bounded $r$.
We do not \emph{solve} Hume; we \emph{relocate} the problem from
justification to bounds.
\end{remark}

\chapter{Classical Logic as an Idealized Projection (L-09)}

First-order logic with bivalence and monotonicity is the \emph{ideal limit}:
$\dot\delta=0$ (a frozen world), $\eta=0$ (a noiseless readout),
$t\to\infty$ (unbounded inference time), whence $r\to0$ exactly and every
proposition is crisply decidable.

\begin{proposition}[Structural isomorphism]\Dr
The relation of classical logic to native logic is isomorphic to the relation
of the continuum to the discrete graph, and of overdamped classical physics
($k<k_c$) to the full dispersion relation, in the parent canon.
\end{proposition}

Classical logic is therefore not \emph{wrong} --- it is extremely accurate in
near-limit regimes (pure mathematics, where $\delta$ is pinned by definition,
is the most near-limit regime there is, which is why FOL flourishes there).
It fails when applied to the live world ($\eta>0$, $\dot\delta\neq0$): the
problem of induction, the paradoxes of material implication, and the failures
of monotonic reasoning in common sense are all symptoms of using a projection
outside its regime.

\section{Sorites: the cut is in the readout}

World-side, there is only a smooth graded quantity $g(n)$; the knower reads it
through a retention threshold $\Pi$ as a binary predicate
$H(n) = [\,g(n)\ge\Pi\,]$.

\begin{theorem}[Sorites relocation --- machine-checked core]\coq\label{th:sorites}
For monotone $g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ and threshold readout $H$:
\emph{(T1)} the readout never flips back; \emph{(T2)} at most one flip point
exists; \emph{(T3)} the ``paradoxical step'' $H(n)\neq H(n{+}1)$ occurs
exactly at the flip point; \emph{(T4)} raising the threshold can only delay
truth. All four are proved in Coq 8.18, axiom-free
(\texttt{Print Assumptions}: \emph{Closed under the global context});
the script is Appendix~B.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\textbf{Operational instantiation (LTP2, \fd):} with
$g(n)=1-e^{-n/1500}$ (smooth: $\max|\Delta g| = 6.7\times10^{-4}$), the
binary readout has exactly one forced jump at $n=1039$ for $\Pi=0.5$; moving
$\Pi$ to $0.8$ moves the jump to $n=2414$ while $g$ is untouched.

\textbf{Conclusion.} The sorites cut is a property of the $\Pi$-readout,
provably not of the graded world --- the price paid when a bounded-bandwidth
system compresses a graded residual into a binary readout. \textbf{Honest
scope:} the Coq theorem covers monotone $g$ (exactly LTP2's regime);
non-monotone $g$ admits multiple flips.

\chapter{Ontology (Stream Steps 01--15)}

``What exists?'' transforms into ``which eigenmodes have not yet decayed?''
Three strongest consequences: (1) \emph{substance ontology falls} --- there
is no ``thing'' beneath properties, only temporarily retained patterns of
difference; mass itself is inverse memory, not stuff. (2) \emph{Impermanence
is a theorem}, not a doctrine: every mode decays,
$|a_k[n]| \le |a_k[0]|\,e^{-\gamma_k n\Delta\theta}$. (3) Several classical
ontological questions become ill-formed --- ``what was before the Big Bang?''
commits the same grammatical error as ``which direction is north of the North
Pole?'' --- and the method operator $\Omega_{\mathrm{phil}}$ (Chapter~22) has
a grammar gate built to catch exactly this class.

\chapter{Epistemology (Steps 18--28)}

Post-Gettier epistemology circles ``justified true belief plus what?''. This
book changes the unit of analysis: knowledge is not a \emph{state} to be
audited for three properties but an \emph{event} of alignment under
resistance. Justification becomes legitimacy conditions: a knowledge claim is
legitimate when (a) real world-resistance is in the loop (no hallucination),
(b) the correction channel is open, and (c) the claim does not exceed its
accessibility class. Foundationalism, coherentism, and reliabilism become
three projections of one condition set --- exactly as the three truth
theories were unified in Chapter~6. \Dr

\begin{remark}[Gettier re-read]\Dr
A Gettier case is a situation where $r$ is low \emph{by accident}: alignment
occurs but is not maintained by genuine coupling to $\theta$. Compare
LTP1-C2: a genuinely tracking system must re-descend when $\delta$ changes; a
system that was ``right by luck'' does not respond. Knowledge differs from
true belief in its \emph{dynamics}, not by one more static property.
\end{remark}

\chapter{Philosophy of Mind (Steps 29--34)}

Direct inheritance from the parent canon: the knower forms \emph{inside} the

\chapter{Philosophy of Science (The Operator Calculus)}

Popper gave falsifiability as a demarcation line but no \emph{calculus} of
where to repair after a refutation; Kuhn gave paradigm shifts but no formal
mechanism. This book supplies both: methodology is an executable operator
loop, and refutation has \emph{coordinates} --- each falsifier points at the
step it breaks. Kuhn's ``crisis'' is the state in which residuals accumulate
while the identifiability gate says parameter repair is insufficient: the
grammar $A$ itself must change. Paradigm $=A$; normal science $=$ descent
under fixed $A$; revolution $=$ change of $A$. \Dr

\begin{principle}[Readout-vs-readout falsification]\Dr\label{prin:rvr}
A refutation benchmarked against an infinity-injected intermediate is
invalid. A valid test must compare readout against readout --- predict the
finite measured quantity directly. This is one notch sharper than bare
Popperianism: it filters \emph{false refutations}, not only false theories.
\end{principle}

The discipline ``identifiability before claiming'' (orbit data identify $GM$,
never $G$ and $M$ separately) is likewise elevated here from good practice to
a binding rule of method --- something mainstream philosophy of science still
lacks as an enforced gate.

\chapter{The Operator $\Omega_{\mathrm{phil}}$: Three Worked Problems}

The solve loop: (1) \textbf{encode} the problem as a difference-question on

\section{Is there free will?}
Grammar gate: if ``free'' means exempt from causal chains, the question is
ill-formed (everything lives on the causal graph). If ``free'' means agency
--- choice under constraint that preserves repair capacity --- it is
well-formed, located at the agency step. Answer \Dr: libertarian free will,
ill-formed; structural-compatibilist free will, real as repair-preserving
agency. Falsifier: exhibit agency functionally identical in every respect
with no internal model load-bearing in the loop.

\section{Is mathematics discovered or invented?}
The grammar gate catches a false dilemma: on the stream the answer is
\emph{both, at different layers}. Difference-structure (e.g.\ spectral truths
of graphs) is \emph{discovered} (world-side, resistant); symbol systems and
idealized limits (FOL, the continuum) are \emph{invented} as good projections
(knower-side). \Dr\ Falsifier: exhibit a mathematical truth with neither
world-resistance nor projection status.

\section{Why is there something rather than nothing?}
Grammar gate: identifying ``absolute nothing'' requires distinguishing it
from ``something'' --- contentful nothing presupposes difference; the very
form ``why $X$ rather than $Y$'' presupposes a field of alternatives, which
is modal difference, which is already something. The question is
\emph{ill-formed in its absolute form}: it eats its own tail. \Dr\ This is
what the primordial axiom gives for free: difference needs no explanation
from something deeper, because any explaining already uses difference.
