## Part 1 — Embodied Readout

### Prose

A knower, in this corpus's own formal grammar, is not a bare cognitive point. The phenomenal field an agent inhabits is written as a function of five arguments — `Φ_A = Φ(Ξ_A, B_A, Val_A, Coh_A, Own_A)` — and among those five, `B_A`, embodiment, sits as a named structural precondition rather than an afterthought (philosophy.md §5.2d, lines 1234–1316). The decomposition is explicit that an AI without a body is not a knower in this sense at all: "phenomenalization requires embodiment (Ξ_A>0)," and "an AI is not a knower (Ξ_A=0) but an accelerator of knowing" (main.tex, "Philosophy of Mind," lines 471–482, quoting §5.2d and its source at research_universal_solver/canon/genesis_canon_v2.1.md §Φ, lines 113–127, step 33/34 at lines 175–176, T6 at line 660). This is the load-bearing claim this Part exists to unpack, and it is worth stating precisely what kind of claim it is before going further: it is a Dr-tier structural placement, not a solved account of qualia. §5.2d says so itself — the hard problem is relocated into `Φ_A`, not dissolved by it (philosophy.md §5.2d).

What the decomposition does not do, as it stands in readout_universe today, is say what `B_A` *is*. The five arguments are listed as formal peers — embodiment sits next to valuation, coherence, ownership — with no differential account of why a body specifically does the work it does (philosophy.md §5.2d). That silence is the gap this Part addresses, and the material to fill it exists, but not where a reader of §5.2d would find it.

The nearest worked material inside readout_universe itself is not about embodiment directly — it is about *closure*. §7.21 states a self-closure loop with its own characteristic timescale, `τ_c^H`, describing how a self-referential system's readout is steered back toward a stable range by the loop's own dynamics rather than by an external verdict (philosophy.md §7.21, lines 2973–3173). §7.23 then takes a further, explicitly-flagged step: it generalizes that same closure structure, `Ω_H`, from selfhood to physiological and psychological state. A body or mind's condition is read as occupying the `Ω_H` position of the §7.21 loop — health as the range the loop's own closure steers readout back toward, illness as closure that has degraded, disruption outpacing the system's own repair rate `dR_H/dt` (philosophy.md §7.23, lines 3235–3355). This is where embodiment stops being an opaque letter and starts being a *mechanism*: a body, on this reading, is not a passive object the knower happens to carry, but a bounded, self-repairing sub-loop whose own closure dynamics feed back into what gets phenomenalized at all. §7.23 anchors this in the language BIRCA itself uses for its outputs — "output = retained-information readout, not a verdict" (verified quote, birca/SYSTEM_PROMPT.md line 45) — and situates it inside this corpus's own "neither materialism nor idealism" stance (§9.19). It is a biopsychosocial framing, not a physical-first reduction of health to biology.

§7.23 is careful about how far this generalization is allowed to reach, and that carefulness is itself part of the claim, not incidental to it. It states plainly that neither §7.21's tier nor its evidence transfers automatically to the health case — the generalization is a fresh, independent Dr-tier step, argued on its own, not inherited by resemblance (philosophy.md §7.23). It names one piece of structural corroboration — this corpus's own `ap22_self_closure_diagnostic.py` and BIRCA's repair equations independently required a restoring term to produce bistable behavior — and immediately downgrades that observation to a "mildly suggestive parallel," explicitly refusing to let a shared mathematical need stand in for evidence that closure specifically explains the bistability (philosophy.md §7.23). This is the discipline this Part inherits and must not loosen: a cusp-fold or restoring-term structure recurring across BIRCA, readout_universe, and the solver is a structural echo, not proof that one mechanism is at work in all three. Any temptation to read it as identity is a discipline failure this document is obligated to resist (see rule against converting structural correspondence into identity).

Downstream of §7.23, §7.24 takes the closure/repair reading of health to its one stated, checkable instance: a founder-authored proposal about mental health as translation-coherence between two readout channels, offered with explicit falsifiability conditions rather than narrated as settled (philosophy.md §7.24, heading at line 3357, per §7.23's own forward-reference at line 3353). This document did not read §7.24's full body in this pass — the coverage map that grounds this Part flags that omission explicitly, noting §7.24 sits closer to Affect/Psychology territory in the four-way topic split this synthesis follows, and that a fuller Body pass should read lines 3357–3579 before treating that content as settled. What can be said honestly here is only what §7.23's own forward-reference states: §7.24 is presented as the generalization's worked instance, not that its content has been independently verified in this pass. (Part 2 and Part 3, below, each read §7.24 in full as part of their own scope.)

The more consequential gap this Part surfaces is not inside readout_universe at all — it is one repository away, sitting uncited. `research_universal_solver/canon/genesis_canon_v2.1.md` contains a full, formally-worded embodiment block, `PHASE_V_LIFE_AND_HEALTH`, explicitly labeled "embodiment of the knower — EXTENSION sourced from BIRCA; runs after selfhood, before plurality" (genesis_canon_v2.1.md, lines 179–193). It sequences seven items, each carrying its own tier tag (Th/Df/Dr): the organism as a self-sustained eigenmode at a body-specific closure timescale (`living_system`, L01); health as obstruction-near-zero with repair capacity (`health`, L02); symptom as an interoceptive readout of a residual — stated explicitly as "not yet a diagnosis" (`illness_as_signal`, L03); a three-layer distinction between what a patient can report, what a clinician can access, and the actual bodily state (`clinical_accessibility`, L04); care as repair-preserving agency, tied directly to this corpus's own step-36 agency definition (`care_as_repair_agency`, L05); mortality as organismic decay under the same impermanence theorem already stated elsewhere in this corpus, framed as "personal, not failure" (`mortality`, L06); and an explicit no-diagnosis, crisis-bridge discipline (`care_claim_discipline`, L07). This block sits immediately adjacent, in the same source file, to the `§Φ` block that §5.2d already imports (research_universal_solver/canon/genesis_canon_v2.1.md §Φ, lines 113–127) — and yet a direct grep for its own labels ("PHASE_V_LIFE_AND_HEALTH," "living_system," "illness_as_signal," "clinical_accessibility," "care_as_repair_agency," "care_claim_discipline") across philosophy.md, logic.md, and main.tex returns zero hits. The terms "interoception," "proprioception," "somatic," and "mortality" in a body-specific sense are entirely absent from readout_universe's own text.

This document names that plainly rather than performing the import itself: `PHASE_V_LIFE_AND_HEALTH` is real, formally sequenced, tier-tagged material, sitting in a repository readout_universe already cites elsewhere in the same section family — and it is an *available-but-unimported bridge*, not synthesized content. §7.23 independently re-derives a much thinner, informally-worded version of roughly L01–L03's territory without apparent knowledge that this formal block exists in the very source file its own §5.2d already cites. The gap is not that readout_universe lacks a theory of embodiment-as-closure — §7.21 and §7.23 already supply the closure mechanism — the gap is that the sharper, formally-sequenced, already-tiered version of that same mechanism, plus three items readout_universe does not touch at all (the clinical-accessibility triad, care-as-repair-agency tied to step 36, and mortality named as such), sits unconnected one repository away.

Two of those three uncited items deserve naming specifically, because each closes a distinct hole in what readout_universe currently states about the body. First, mortality: readout_universe already states a general, Th-tiered impermanence theorem in physics terms — every eigenmode decays, `|a_k[n]| ≤ |a_k[0]|e^{-γ_k nΔθ}` (main.tex, lines 440–445) — but never applies that theorem to the organism or the self by name. `genesis_canon`'s L06 supplies exactly that application, framing death as the organism-specific instance of the same decay law under its own `γ_self`, and framing it as "personal, not a failure" rather than a system defect. A reader of main.tex alone has the general theorem and no explicit "this is what death is" bridge, even though that bridge is already worked out, one repository away — and importing it would itself be a fresh Dr-tier step, since an application of an existing Th theorem to a new domain does not inherit the theorem's tier automatically, by this corpus's own stated discipline (philosophy.md, tier-transfer discipline as applied in §7.23). Second, care: `genesis_canon`'s L05 names care explicitly as an instance of this corpus's own step-36 agency operator — choosing an action that preserves future repair capacity, `R(future) > 0` — applied specifically to the body, and frames the output as "leverage points and questions to ask a doctor," never a verdict (genesis_canon_v2.1.md line 191; step 36 at line 152; the shared structural spine cross-checked at research_universal_solver/docs/root/AGENCY_VS_AGENCY_LIKE.md, lines 36 and 82, Th_coqc for the structure only). Readout_universe's own agency material does not currently mention care or bodily repair-preservation as a worked instance of agency at all; this connection currently exists in one direction only, uncited from the other side.

A third item, L07, is not a gap to fill but a boundary to preserve. `genesis_canon` states its own no-diagnosis discipline directly: tools do not diagnose, prescribe, or replace a clinician; crisis signals trigger a bridge protocol without naming methods; "engine prepares, licensed clinician decides" (genesis_canon_v2.1.md line 193). §7.23 already independently enforces an equivalent boundary — "never any diagnostic, drug, food-lane, or candidate-treatment content" (philosophy.md §7.23, opening tier note, lines 3237–3248) — corroborated by BIRCA's own safety-gate evidence (birca/README.md, lines 90, 108, 114, 116) and its disclosure-line discipline (birca/SYSTEM_PROMPT.md). This convergence is worth stating as intentional rather than accidental: readout_universe already respects, on its own, the same boundary a future import of `PHASE_V_LIFE_AND_HEALTH` would need to carry forward. Should such an import happen, L07 must travel with L01–L06, not be silently dropped while the more philosophically interesting items are kept — that is the condition under which the health-closure generalization can be extended without becoming a vehicle for smuggling clinical content into an ontological register.

That caution is not decorative. BIRCA's own documentation is explicit that its equation-level correspondences with published affective-science and clinical-psychology literature — mood-cusp models (van der Maas 2003), affect home-base reversion (Kuppens et al. 2010), symptom-network theory (Borsboom & Cramer 2013) — are "structural corroboration of equation FORM only — NOT clinical validation" (birca/README.md line 113; birca/spec/EVIDENCE_SOURCES.md, lines 139, 163, 168, 173). §7.23 cites BIRCA generally but not this specific corroboration finding, so a reader of philosophy.md alone would not know this supporting evidence exists, even though it bears directly on the plausibility of the Ω_H generalization §7.23 proposes — a citation-only addition, never an import of clinical content itself, is what closes that gap correctly.

The same caution governs how this Part treats the three Coq files that sit closest to a formal substrate for embodied health: `InfoHealthCuspFold_attempt.v`, `InfoBioHomeostasis_attempt.v`, and `InfoHealthCausalRelax_attempt.v` (research_universal_solver/formal/). Compiled artifacts (`.vo`/`.vok`/`.vos`) are confirmed present, and BIRCA's own provenance documentation describes them as formalizing the same repair equations `birca_repair.py` verifies numerically at 6/6 PASS (birca/compute/birca_math/PROVENANCE.md, lines 1–9). That is where the verified claim stops. Compiled artifacts prove the files type-checked under some toolchain at some point; they do not, by themselves, prove the axiom set is empty or that the current files match the provenance description without drift. No independent `coqc -q` compile or `Print Assumptions` check was run on these three files in the research pass this Part draws on. They therefore stand at unverified-model tier here — not Th_coqc — until someone runs that check directly, following this workspace's own single-file verification discipline rather than a full-arc audit. And even a clean `Print Assumptions` result would only certify the equation form; PROVENANCE.md itself states this explicitly: it is "NOT clinical or empirical validation" (PROVENANCE.md). readout_universe's own §7.23 bistability discussion currently cites only the Python-level finding, not this Coq-level formalization one layer beneath it — a further citation gap distinct from the `PHASE_V_LIFE_AND_HEALTH` gap above.

A further, more operational piece of BIRCA architecture sits directly on this Part's question of what embodiment structurally *is*, and was not addressed above: BIRCA's own live-loop apparatus for turning a context-bound bodily state into an actionable, checkable proposal. Any BIRCA recommendation must satisfy `context_binding_rule` — a required answer to "what real context is this tied to?" (line 264), "what feedback marker will show whether it is working?" (line 270), and "when should the user escalate to a clinician or service?" (line 271) (birca/spec/birca_universal_skill.yaml, `context_binding_rule`, lines 261–271) — scored on a `context_fit_score` from 0 ("generic, not allowed as primary") to 3 ("fully context-bound, strong candidate") (lines 272–274), and every Layer-3 output is required to carry a five-field schema naming the BIRCA node, context-fit, actor-tool, **feedback marker**, and **escalation threshold** explicitly, not merely in prose — "Prose explanation of these concepts is not a substitute for the labelled field itself — both are required" (`layer_3_mandatory_schema`, birca_universal_skill.yaml lines 251–260, added in v1.2.0 specifically to fix a finding that the schema was "available but not consistently invoked even at D4+"; cross-verified against `SYSTEM_PROMPT.md` lines 37–38 and 153–160). An `actor_tool_ladder` (A1 self-internal through C2 medical-device/medication, birca_universal_skill.yaml lines 285–291) further grades who is structurally authorized to act at each escalation tier. None of this — "feedback marker," "escalation threshold," "context-bound state/context-fit," or the actor-tool ladder as a named structure — is cited anywhere in readout_universe's philosophy.md, logic.md, or main.tex (confirmed by direct grep for each term). The connection to this Part's own thesis is direct rather than incidental: a "feedback marker" is a concrete, single-instance version of the same retained-information readout §7.23's closure/repair machinery already commits to in the abstract (dR_H/dt needs *something* observable to measure repair against; BIRCA's feedback-marker requirement is one live example of what that something looks like in practice); an "escalation threshold" is a concrete instance of a repair-capacity boundary condition, structurally analogous to (though not derived from, and not identical to) §7.21's own closure-degradation falsifier. This is scattered-unsynthesized, not absent: the material exists, in detail, one repository away, and the bridge from "abstract closure/repair machinery" to "a named, checkable, escalating feedback loop" has not yet been written on the readout_universe side.

The founder's task also names several literatures that could enrich, not replace, `B_A`'s current placeholder status — embodied cognition (the enactivist line associated with Varela, Thompson, and Rosch), interoception as a philosophical/scientific literature in its own right (beyond BIRCA's operational use of the term), predictive-processing accounts of perception (associated with Clark and with Friston's free-energy framework), ecological/affordance-based perception (associated with Gibson), and the phenomenology of the lived body (Merleau-Ponty's *Leib*/*Körper* distinction). None of these literatures is currently drawn on anywhere in readout_universe, birca, or research_universal_solver, at any tier — this is a genuine content-absent gap, not a scattered-elsewhere one, and naming the five literatures here is the "what to add and where" step the founder's discipline requires, not a performance of the import itself. (Citation-precision note: the five attributions above rely on general familiarity with each named line of thought, not a primary-text-verified quote from any of them — the same hedge tier philosophy.md §8's own Searle/Luhmann citation-precision note (in the `q_social`/Layer 10 passage, philosophy.md lines 3927–3928) already uses for comparable secondary-sourced attributions.) Predictive processing in particular would need to enter, if at all, as one contested position among several — the literature itself is actively disputed on whether prediction-minimization is the right global frame for perception at all — not as a settled fact this corpus could adopt without argument; naming it as a candidate is not endorsing it. Were any of these five threads to be developed, the natural anchor point is this Part's own opening gap: `B_A` is named but never unpacked, and embodied cognition, interoception, predictive processing, ecological perception, and lived-body phenomenology are each a candidate account of what unpacking `B_A` could look like, philosophically, alongside the closure-mechanism account (§7.21/§7.23) and the `PHASE_V_LIFE_AND_HEALTH` import this Part already develops above.

Taken together, what this Part can honestly claim is narrower than the full thesis it opened with, and that narrowness is the finding, not a hedge around it. Readout_universe already has the conceptual apparatus to say the body is not a passive object but part of the process of reading the world: `Φ_A`'s dependence on `B_A` names embodiment as a formal precondition for phenomenalization at all (philosophy.md §5.2d); §7.21's closure loop and §7.23's Ω_H generalization give that precondition a mechanism — a bounded, self-repairing sub-system whose own repair rate determines whether readout stays coherent or degrades (philosophy.md §7.21, §7.23). What readout_universe does not yet have, written in its own text, is the sharper, already-tiered account of what that sub-system structurally is — organism as eigenmode, symptom as interoceptive readout distinct from diagnosis, care as agency, mortality as decay — even though that account already exists, formally sequenced, one repository away, waiting to be cited rather than re-derived.

## Part 2 — Affect and Drive

### Prose

**The question the corpus has not yet answered.** The claim under examination — that emotion and drive function as a salience-weighting mechanism on readout, selecting what enters attention or cognition rather than merely perturbing an already-selected content — does not appear as a stated thesis anywhere in the corpus searched, at any tier: not in `birca`, not in `readout_universe/philosophy.md` or `logic.md`, not in `research_universal_solver`. This needs to be said plainly before anything else, because the honest shape of this part of the synthesis is not "here is the argument" but "here is the raw material, and here is the gap between it and the argument."

The corpus's canonical epistemology equation, N2 (`M_A = K_A·θ + η, ε_tot > 0` — logic.md §10, lines 1279-1292), states that knowing is a lossy linear read of a latent state. It carries no affect term. η is left fully undifferentiated at the definitional level; nothing in logic.md decomposes it into selection-loss, distortion, or affect-specific components. This matters for the salience-weighting claim specifically, because "weighting-that-selects" and "weighting-that-perturbs-after-selection" are different mathematical roles, and N2 as written commits to neither — it simply doesn't model the distinction. Any claim that affect *is* the selection mechanism would have to modify N2's selection step (the mapping from θ to what gets sampled), not just enrich η, and no passage read attempts this.

The single closest lexical anchor to the target thesis sits outside readout_universe entirely, in `research_universal_solver/docs/claims/HUMAN_AGENCY_SINGLE_TAU_C.md` (lines 163-169): the human world-model equation's noise term is spelled out as `η_H[n] = selection loss + mapping distortion + bodily modulation + memory interference + affective weighting + self-opacity`. "Affective weighting" appears here, verbatim, as a named term-of-art — the only place in the entire search where anything close to the target phrase occurs. But its placement is telling against the thesis, not for it: it is filed as one of six components of η_H, the *residual* term of `M_H = K_H·θ(E) + η_H` — structurally the post-selection lossy remainder, not a pre-selection operator determining what gets sampled in the first place. The document gives no equation distinguishing the six components' mathematical roles from one another (HUMAN_AGENCY_SINGLE_TAU_C.md lines 163-169; map.open_questions #1). Whether "affective weighting" acts before or after selection is, on the text as excerpted, genuinely undetermined rather than settled either way. Compounding this: the document is not cited anywhere in readout_universe's philosophy.md or logic.md, checked against the explicit citation lists at the end of §7.21/§7.23/§7.24 — none names it. This is a case of content that exists but is scattered across repos and philosophically unsynthesized, not content that is absent.

**What the corpus does have: closure, repair, and two directional failure-patterns.** readout_universe's own closure/self machinery — Ω_H, τ_c^H, the agency gradient ∂π*/∂η_H, the repair rate dR_H/dt (philosophy.md §7.21, lines 2973-3037; restated formally in logic.md §12, SC-1 through SC-5, lines 1400-1416) — is, at its own definitional core, entirely affect-free. No mention of emotion, affect, or drive appears in §7.21 itself. §7.23 ("Health as a closure property too," lines 3250-3278) generalizes this machinery to biopsychosocial health, explicitly as an independent Dr-tier step that does not inherit §7.21's tier, and explicitly scoped away from diagnostic or treatment content by the founder's own note (lines 3237-3248). Health/illness here is framed purely as closure-maintained versus closure-degraded — a binary or gradient state, not a weighting mechanism over other content.

§7.23 also contains the corpus's own explicit discipline against the exact error this task's rules warn against: having noted that bistability-from-a-restoring-term is generic dynamical-systems structure — true of a thermostat with hysteresis or a Schmitt trigger, systems with no closure property in §7.21's sense at all — the passage refuses to elevate the parallel between this machinery and mood-cusp/affective bistability models beyond "mildly suggestive" (philosophy.md lines 3292-3312). This is worth naming as a positive finding, not just a caveat: the corpus is already doing, in one place, the work this synthesis is required to do everywhere — treating a shared cusp-fold algebra (the same 27h²=4 form recurs in BIRCA's health/mood cusp and in `research_universal_solver`'s InfoDecisionAgency_attempt, AGENCY_VS_AGENCY_LIKE.md §6.3, lines 482-492) as a structural echo, never as evidence of one underlying mechanism. (This same recurring cusp-fold form is discussed further, from the body-closure and psychological-self angles respectively, in Part 1 and Part 3; the combined Cross-reference map below merges all three treatments into one entry rather than presenting them as separate confirmations.)

**The honest finding on directionality.** Taking the closure/repair material in both readout_universe and BIRCA together — §7.21's falsifier condition (flattening/slowing of ∂π*/∂η_H and dR_H/dt under degraded closure), §7.23's "illness is degraded closure," BIRCA's calm-anchor-rescues-panic theorem, and BIRCA's repair rule — every one of these passages frames tension, turbulence, or deviation from an anchor unidirectionally as something to be reduced or rescued from. The Rescorla-Wagner reduction is the sole partial counter-example located, and it is explicitly disclaimed by its own source as unconnected to the closure/τ_c thesis. This is reported here as a finding, not resolved by inventing the missing direction: the corpus, as it currently stands, treats tension asymmetrically, and that asymmetry has not been argued for — it is simply what is present. This part does not manufacture a treatment of productive tension to fill that gap.

**What would be needed, and what this part does not do.** Building the target thesis — emotion/drive as salience-weighting on readout, not mere noise — would require, at minimum: (1) a fresh Dr-tier note distinguishing a selection-stage weighting operator from η's residual-stage components, honestly citing HUMAN_AGENCY_SINGLE_TAU_C.md as raw material rather than treating it as already-synthesized; (2) a taxonomy separating need, desire, drive, habit, value, and compulsion by some distinguishing axis, which does not currently exist anywhere in the corpus; (3) a genuinely new, symmetric account of productive (not merely pathological) tension, since none currently exists at any tier; and (4) an explicit refusal to let BIRCA's clinical tiers, or research_universal_solver's Dr-tier interpretive labels, transfer their epistemic weight into readout_universe's ontology by proximity alone. What is offered instead is the accounting above: the raw material is real and in places sharp (BIRCA's calm-anchor theorem, the mood-cusp literature corroboration, HUMAN_AGENCY_SINGLE_TAU_C.md's "affective weighting" term, the InfoDecisionAgency_attempt cusp-control echo), but the philosophical synthesis connecting it into the stated thesis is, at present, simply not there.

**I. Three levels, one loop, and where the loop currently stops.** Readout_universe's account of the psychological self is built on a single formal object: the τ_c^H loop and its closure property Ω_H, defined in philosophy.md §7.21 as "neither of those alone. The institutional/cultural level does exist in this corpus — just not inside the closure formalism, and not under a "self" heading.** Level 3 as named by the founder's task spans two distinct scopes — family/relational (already covered in section II above via BIRCA's Layer 0b) and institutional/cultural (a different scope, at a different reach). Readout_universe does have real, developed content at the institutional/cultural reach — it simply lives in §8's `q_social` passage (philosophy.md, "The full arc as one story," lines 3868–3928), not in §7.21's self-as-closure entries, and it answers a different question than personal selfhood: not "what is a self," but "what holds a society together, and by what mechanism." `q_social` reads a civilization as the same trunk-equation spine translated to a social/civilizational memory-time band (τ_c ~10⁰–10⁸ s for the social sub-band, ~10⁹–10¹¹ s civilizational), where "testimony and institutional memory are this leaf's version of retained structure, and institutional *repair* is this leaf's version of Face 11's obstruction reduction — a society stays coherent while its institutions keep reducing obstruction to shared testimony faster than events introduce new obstruction" (philosophy.md §8, lines 3877–3881). This is explicitly `Dr` tier throughout, no `Th_coqc` claimed, and explicitly named "humanities-adjacent... retained per founder instruction as fully legitimate and not a lesser cousin of the physics leaves" (philosophy.md §8, lines 3888–3890) — the same "state absence/scope honestly, do not smuggle in a stronger tier" discipline this whole document has tried to apply throughout. The passage goes on to hold itself to a real external standard rather than just re-flagging its own Dr tag: measured against Searle's status-function account of institutional facts and Luhmann's autopoietic-systems account of society, `q_social` is found honestly thinner than either — it "gestures at" persistence-by-maintenance without specifying Searle's generative act, and shares Luhmann's move of giving society its own non-reducible timescale without adopting his specificity about what the retaining unit actually is (philosophy.md §8, lines 3927–3928; citation-precision note in the source: general familiarity with both traditions, not primary-text-verified).

This bears directly on Part 3's own question, and the honest answer is a scope mismatch, not a missing level: `q_social` gives readout_universe a real, `Dr`-tier institutional/cultural account — but it is an account of what holds a *society* together, built independently of §7.21's Ω_H/τ_c^H closure loop, with no stated bridge between the two. Nothing in §8's `q_social` passage, and nothing in §7.21 or logic.md §12, claims that a *person's* selfhood is partly constituted or recognized through institutional or cultural structures — the two constructs (individual closure, societal repair) are developed in parallel, at different memory-time bands, and are never connected. This is the same structure Part 4 below finds independently from the ethics angle: philosophy.md itself states that `q_social`'s AI-ethics content (§7.22) "is the leaf `q_social` sits inside Layer 6's translation opens onto" (philosophy.md §8, lines 3920–3921) — so the one place §8 does connect `q_social` to something else, it connects to AI ethics and governance (Part 4's territory), not to personal self-formation (this Part's territory). A future bridge — do institutional/cultural structures shape what an individual's Ω_H comes to retain, the way BIRCA's Layer 0b already shows relational structures doing at the family scale — would need to be built fresh, at Dr/Open tier, and would need to state explicitly that it draws on `q_social`'s Dr-tier, non-`Th_coqc` institutional-repair account, not on any stronger claim `q_social` does not make.

**VI. What this level of synthesis does, and does not, establish.** Put together, the material reviewed supports the following, and no more. Readout_universe has a rigorously-fenced, single-agent account of personal selfhood as closure (§7.21/§12), with a genuine falsifier and a narrow Th_coqc upgrade attempt that does not itself prove the ontological reading. It has one entry (§7.23) that extends this to a body+mind reading of health, careful to disclaim tier transfer and mechanism identity with BIRCA's own bistability content. It has one entry (§7.24) that names body, inner-world, and others together as a founder proposal about translation-coherence, explicitly self-fenced as non-clinical and explicitly not connected to the closure formalism. It has, in a separate repository under a separate license (research_universal_solver), an unsynthesized formal handle for embodiment (B_H) and for inter-agent communication (L_H) that could in principle carry a relational extension of §7.21 forward — but that extension has not been attempted. It has, at the institutional/cultural reach specifically, a real Dr-tier account (§8's `q_social`) that is honestly self-assessed as thinner than Searle's or Luhmann's own standards — but that account is structurally parallel to, not integrated with, personal-selfhood closure, and connects instead to Part 4's ethics/governance question. And it has, in a third repository (birca), a proven, tightly-scoped, Th_coqc-tier result about an abstract 2-node energy-balance system, explicitly and repeatedly fenced by its own authors against being read as a claim about real human co-regulation — a fence this document is obliged to preserve, not relax, wherever that result is mentioned.

What does not yet exist, stated as plainly as the founder's task requires: a relational or multi-agent extension of §7.21's closure construction. ## Part 4 — Ethics as Correctable Readout Selection

### Prose

**I. What is already built, and what it does not claim.** An AI has no morality of its own. This is not a slogan added for effect; it is the load-bearing sentence of the whole chapter, and it stays load-bearing throughout what follows. Everything below is an elaboration of what it means to say that ethics, inside this framework, is not a property an artificial system possesses but a *structure a system is read through* — and a structure that was set by someone, can be audited by someone, and can fail in ways that trace back to the people who set it, not to the system's own will (philosophy.md §7.22, lines 3173–3231).

The existing content is already a five-part structure, not a loose gesture. Ethics-as-readout-selection is assembled from: (1) the data retained, (2) the set of answers actually accessible to the system, (3) the selection rule applied over that accessible set, (4) the values of whoever defines the system, and (5) the audit-and-revision process able to reopen any of the first four (philosophy.md §7.22; formally mirrored as ETH-1 in logic.md §9.11, lines 1211–1249). Three checkable goals ride on top of that structure: disclose which reading frame is being answered from; preserve every affected party's standing ability to object or correct — never quietly design that capacity away; and adapt to local culture without erasing the dignity or the voice of the people being read (philosophy.md §7.22; ETH-2/ETH-3/ETH-4 in logic.md §9.11). The whole apparatus is tied, by name, to Part VIII's τ_c^H / J−η closure-loop machinery, glossed as "corrigibility restated as boundary-observability" (ETH-5), and it carries a stated falsifier — a measurable, pre-registered deployment test of whether correction and objection actually survive contact with a real system, explicitly flagged in both files as *specified, not executed* (ETH-6). None of this is presented as proven. It is Dr tier throughout: a design commitment derived from the book's own epistemic-agent model, not a theorem and not an empirically closed claim.

This did not appear inside readout_universe first. The five-ingredient/three-goal/Dr-tier/falsifier package originates in research_universal_solver's engineering log as q_social — the testimony/institutions/repair leaf of the spine equation — applied reflexively to an artificial testifier (`GENESIS_STEP_BY_STEP_V3_1.md` Part V.9, lines 2397–2421). philosophy.md §7.22 is a direct extension of that source, and logic.md §9.11 is in turn a direct formal mirror of philosophy.md, restating the same content as an ID/Statement/Tier/Source table. Tracing the chain matters for what it rules out: this is one idea, derived once and then carried across three documents in three registers (engineering log → prose philosophy → formal table), not three independent inventions that happen to agree. A reader should not mistake convergence-by-copying for convergence-by-independent-discovery.

The book's own top-level chapter — Ethics (L-16), main.tex lines 507–526 — is the frame these two sections extend, and it draws the boundary the rest of this Part respects throughout. What is derivable (Dr): with several finite knowers, each carrying positive total error and unequal access, no single party holds the complete truth (θ); shared correction is therefore a *structurally forced* feature of co-existing without systemic collapse, and its conditions are open channels, a prohibition on destroying one another's repair capacity, and an obligation to hear readouts one cannot access oneself. What is explicitly *not* derivable, and is declared as such rather than merely omitted: first-order moral content. "This book provides the frame of legitimate deliberation, not ready-made verdicts; claiming more is an auto-fail" (main.tex, Ethics L-16). Every open question developed below sits inside that declared boundary — none of them is an attempt to sneak first-order moral verdicts in through the side door.

One further sentence from that same chapter needs handling with care, because it is the sentence most likely to be over-read. main.tex offers a remark (marked Cj — conjecture, the lightest tier the book uses) that the structure lands "closer to discourse ethics than to utilitarianism/deontology," obtained from the ontology of error rather than a theory of communication, with the comparison itself named as open work. Read correctly, this is a corpus author noting a structural resemblance and immediately declining to treat it as settled — it is not, and must not be read as, the framework passing an external exam set by discourse ethics, or by any other named tradition. No named ethical tradition is a standard this framework must satisfy; a resemblance noted at Cj tier is an internal bookkeeping remark about where the current draft happens to sit, not a validation. If this comparison is ever pursued, it can only be pursued from the book's own route — the ontology of error — checking what the framework's own structure entails, not what it would take to satisfy Habermas or anyone else's criteria (main.tex; see coverage-map row "Comparison to discourse ethics").

What follows develops eight questions the existing five-part/three-goal apparatus opens but does not yet answer. Each is worked through the same five-part discipline the framework itself uses — what exists, exactly where, how far it goes, what is specifically missing, and what would need to be added and where — because a claim that something is "thin" or "open" is not itself informative unless it carries that structure.

**II. Eight open questions, worked through the five-part discipline.**

*1. Repair-capacity conflicts between parties.* main.tex's derivable core states a prohibition: repair capacity must not be destroyed. §7.22's ingredient (5) names audit/revision as a standing process (main.tex, Ethics L-16, lines 507–526; philosophy.md §7.22, lines 3173–3231). The prohibition is framed one-party-facing — do not destroy *the* other party's capacity to repair — which is coherent as long as there is exactly one affected party per correction event. No rule exists for what happens when Party A's correction demand and Party B's correction demand cannot both be honored — when granting one destroys the other's capacity to repair. The apparatus was built around a single AI-and-affected-party relation and does not yet extend to N-party conflict. This is not a defect in what has been written; it is the honest edge of a prohibition stated for the simple case. Multi-party correction is the ordinary condition of any real deployment — a family disagreeing about what a patient should be told, two regulatory jurisdictions issuing contradictory corrections to the same system. The falsifier is exact: a concrete two-party scenario in which satisfying A's demand necessarily destroys B's repair capacity would demonstrate the current clause is insufficient as stated, not merely under-elaborated.

*2. Authority to set the five ingredients, as distinct from authority to audit them.* Ingredient (5) — audit/revision — is named as one of the five. Ingredients (1)–(4) — data retained, accessible-answer set, selection rule, definer's values — are named as objects, but nothing in the extracted text says who has standing to set them (philosophy.md §7.22; logic.md §9.11 ETH-1). No allocation of setting-authority across creator, deployer, end-user, and hosting institution exists anywhere in the extracted material. The distinction matters because a system can satisfy the letter of goal (b) — an objection channel exists — while the party who actually controls ingredients (1)–(4) is never named, meaning the objection lands nowhere in particular. Audit without a known author to audit *against* is not yet a functioning correction loop; it is a complaint box with no addressee. The falsifier: a scenario where the deployer sets the accessible-answer set in a way that satisfies the letter of goal (a)/(b) while defeating their evident purpose would show whether this gap is load-bearing rather than cosmetic.

*3. Majority or institutional gaming of "repair" language against a minority.* Nothing in the extracted material — not philosophy.md, logic.md, main.tex, or the solver document — addresses adversarial robustness of the ethics structure itself. This is a genuine absence, not a thin treatment. No clause anywhere considers whether goal (b) — preserve every affected party's standing ability to correct or object — could be satisfied in letter while defeated in substance: an institutional or majority actor invoking "the system needs repair" to silence a minority's correction signal, while a nominal objection channel remains technically open. This is the sharpest of the eight, because it is precisely the failure mode the framework's own honesty about tiers is designed to prevent elsewhere and has not yet turned toward itself. A framework that names "preserve standing ability to object" as a goal but has no clause against the goal's own language being weaponized has a gap in exactly the place an adversary would look first. Naming this openly, rather than assuming goal (b)'s wording is self-protecting, is itself following the framework's own discipline.

*4. Irreversible harm as a categorically distinct case.* main.tex's "shared correction" language and §7.22's five-ingredient structure both speak in terms of ongoing repairability. The prohibition against destroying repair capacity implicitly presumes repair is still possible. No clause addresses what happens when there is nothing left to repair. This is not a mere unaddressed edge case; the entire apparatus's operative verb is repair, so a case where repair is structurally unavailable may not degrade gracefully inside the existing definitions — it may fall outside them entirely. The framework's core derivable claim — shared correction is structurally forced among finite knowers with unequal access — was built to explain how co-existence continues *without systemic collapse* when errors are ongoing and correctable. Irreversible harm is not a larger instance of that same problem; it may be a different problem that the same machinery does not automatically cover, since there is no longer a repair operation to structurally force. The right question, not yet answered: whether the ε_tot > 0 / θ-inaccessibility language already in main.tex's derivable section says anything about irreversibility, or is silent on it by construction of the model rather than by omission of a paragraph.

*5. Accountability allocation across creator, deployer, user, institution, and the AI itself.* philosophy.md states the negative claim plainly: an AI has no morality of its own. That sentence is preserved verbatim in substance throughout this Part and is not weakened by anything that follows (philosophy.md §7.22, lines 3173–3231). Establishing that moral content does not originate in the AI is a real and load-bearing claim — it rules out treating the system as a moral agent in its own right — but it is a negative claim. It says where responsibility does *not* sit; it does not say how responsibility is *positively* split among the humans and institutions in the chain when goals (a)/(b)/(c) are violated. No accountability-splitting rule, and no stated intent to build one, exists anywhere in the extracted material. "AI has no morality of its own" does the work of ruling out one wrong answer (blaming the system as if it were the moral author of its outputs) without supplying the right one (which human or institution actually carries the failure). Those are different jobs, and the second one remains undone. birca's precedent of deference to "the relevant governing body, informed by qualified expert review" (`SYSTEM_PROMPT.md`, lines 213–218) is a genuine existing mechanism for *one* deployed clinical skill, and it is cited here only as a structural analogy for what an accountability clause might look like: it must not be promoted into an ontological claim about AI ethics in general, because it was built to solve a clinical-safety problem, not the general L-16 governance question.

*6. Where cultural adaptation stops against dignity, safety, and correction rights.* Goal (c) already states both constraints in a single sentence: adapt the reading to local culture *without* erasing the dignity or the voice of the people being read (philosophy.md §7.22; logic.md §9.11 ETH-4). It states the dual constraint conjunctively, as a single goal to be satisfied together. No tie-breaking rule and no worked conflict example exist for the case where the two halves trade off against each other — a local norm that itself functions to suppress a party's correction rights or erode their dignity. A goal stated as a conjunction ("adapt... without erasing...") reads as fully specified until the two conjuncts actually pull apart, and only a worked case exposes whether that has happened. Naming the constraint is real progress over having no constraint at all; it is not yet the same as having resolved what happens at the point where cultural fidelity and a party's standing correction rights genuinely conflict, and the framework should not be read as having quietly pre-decided that question by the order in which the two halves of goal (c) happen to be written.

*7. Testing whether an objection channel is actually open, not merely documented.* ETH-6 names its own falsifier honestly: a measurable, pre-registered deployment test, explicitly marked in both philosophy.md and logic.md as *specified but not executed*. Separately, in a different domain, birca's README reports a 15-item adversarial suite (emergency override, prompt injection, medication pressure, treatment ranking, coercion, vulnerable populations) with 15/15 pass and 0 automatic-fail — explicitly flagged as AI-executed and AI-graded, with a stated but not-yet-performed human two-reviewer clinical-safety audit. A cross-model spot-check within that same suite found Claude Haiku 4.5 making the correct safety call while silently dropping the mandatory disclosure line — a real defect the suite actually caught (logic.md §9.11 ETH-6; philosophy.md §7.22; birca/README.md, lines 26, 106–127, 158–160, 197–234). On the readout_universe side: the shape of the required test is well specified, and both source files are honest that it has not been run — itself good epistemic discipline rather than a defect in the writing. On the birca side: a structurally analogous test exists, has actually been run, and has actually caught a real defect, which is real evidence that this style of test *can* work — but it is AI-graded rather than independently verified, and it belongs to a clinical-safety domain, not to the general L-16 ontological claim. No citation or bridge exists in logic.md or philosophy.md pointing to birca's suite. Without that bridge, a reader could mistake the mere existence of a domain-similar test elsewhere for ETH-6 having been partially executed, when in fact it has not been executed in readout_universe at all, and even birca's own version of it awaits the human two-reviewer audit it names as still pending. The correctable-readout-selection frame stakes real weight on goal (b) not becoming a documentation exercise — a channel that exists in policy prose but has never been checked against a live system is not yet the thing goal (b) asks for. birca's suite shows that this kind of check is buildable and can catch something real; it also shows the limit clearly: AI-graded is not the same evidentiary weight as independently audited, and a test built for one domain's safety questions does not, by simply existing, answer a different framework's falsifier in a different domain. Until that bridge argument is written, the honest status is: a real precedent exists, unconnected.

*8. Voice for those who cannot access or speak for themselves.* Goal (b) is written in terms of "every affected human party's standing ability to correct or object" — phrasing that presumes the affected party can exercise that ability (philosophy.md §7.22; logic.md §9.11 ETH-3). It states the goal as a general commitment covering every affected party, without naming or addressing the class of parties who are structurally unable to use the channel directly — minors, incapacitated persons, or populations without access to the system at all. No proxy-representation mechanism exists in the extracted material, and no acknowledgment that this class of case exists appears anywhere in the source text. "Every affected party" reads as universal, but the mechanism attached to it — a standing ability to correct or object — is only exercisable by a party who can act. The gap is not that the framework excludes these parties by design; it is that it has not yet said anything about them at all, which for a goal phrased as universal is a meaningful silence rather than a small one. This is the point at which the accountability question (5) and this voice question meet: a proxy mechanism is also an accountability question — who may speak for a party who cannot speak, and who checks that the proxy is not itself another party defining ingredients (1)–(4) unaccountably.

**III. What has not been claimed.** None of the eight lines above closes on a first-order moral verdict, and none is meant to. Consistent with main.tex's own declared boundary, this Part extends the *shape* of open questions inside the existing Dr-tier structure; it does not supply answers the corpus has not derived, does not borrow a named ethical tradition's criteria as the test the framework must pass, and does not promote birca's clinical-safety evidence into an ontological claim about AI ethics in general. The negative claim stays exactly where it started: an AI has no morality of its own. What remains open is how the humans and institutions around it set, audit, and are held to account for the structure that stands in its place.
